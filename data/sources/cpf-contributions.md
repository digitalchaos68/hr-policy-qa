# How Do I Pay My CPF Contributions?

As an employee, you need not do anything as your employer is required to pay the employer's and employee's share of CPF contributions to the CPF Board on a monthly basis. Your employer will deduct the employee's share of CPF contributions from your wages.

Whether CPF contributions are payable depends on total wages per month:

- Total wages up to $50: no employer contribution, no employee contribution.
- Total wages above $50 to $500: employer contribution payable, no employee contribution.
- Total wages above $500: both employer and employee contributions payable.

For employees aged 55 and below earning more than $750 a month, as a Singapore Citizen or Singapore Permanent Resident (3rd year onwards), the combined employer and employee CPF contribution rate is 37% of Ordinary Wages (subject to the prevailing Ordinary Wage Ceiling) plus 37% of Additional Wages, of which 20 percentage points is the employee's share. Contribution rates are lower for first- and second-year Permanent Residents, and are graduated down for employees above age 55, reflecting CPF Board's published age-based contribution tables.

CPF contribution rates and wage ceilings are reviewed and may change periodically. Employers should use the official CPF Contribution Calculator on cpf.gov.sg to confirm the exact rates and ceilings in effect for the relevant month, rather than relying solely on a cached summary.

*Content summarized from the CPF Board's "How do I pay my CPF contributions?" article and published CPF contribution rate tables, retrieved October 2026.*
